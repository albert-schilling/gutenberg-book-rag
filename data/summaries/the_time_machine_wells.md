The book is a speculative fiction narrative presented as a series of lectures and a personal journal. It opens with the Time Traveller addressing a small group of friends—Filby, the Medical Man, the Psychologist, the Editor—about a new theory of time and a machine he has built. He then recounts his own voyage, describing the incomplete device, its activation, and his arrival in a distant future where humanity has split into two fragile races. The delicate, child‑like Eloi live in elegant, decaying palaces and fear darkness; the subterranean Morlocks maintain the machinery and prey on the Eloi. The Traveller befriends a young Eloi woman, Weena, explores ruined structures such as the Palace of Green Porcelain and a yellow‑metal seat overlooking a silent Thames, and gradually discovers the grim reality behind the apparent utopia. As darkness spreads—an eclipse or planetary transit—the world falls silent, hinting at the extinction of humanity’s intellect. The narrative ends with the narrator waiting for the Traveller’s return; three years have passed and the Traveller has vanished, leaving only speculation whether he fell into prehistoric ages or moved further forward. The story is built from the Traveller’s spoken account, interspersed with the narrator’s reflections, and concludes with an open‑ended epilogue.
