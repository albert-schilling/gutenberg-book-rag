Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus is an epistolary gothic novel composed of letters, framed narratives, and interwoven chapters that recount a series of tragic events across Europe. The story opens with a letter from a traveler in St. Petersburgh, expressing enthusiasm for a northern expedition, and soon shifts to Victor Frankenstein’s childhood, his education under the benevolent Professor Waldman, and his growing fascination with chemistry and the limits of nature. Victor’s family life is described through letters from his cousin Elizabeth and accounts of his father’s health, while a secondary plot follows the misfortunes of the Beaufort family, whose fall into poverty and hidden existence illustrate themes of pride and decline. Victor’s secret studies lead him to a remote cottage where he steals food, discovers books such as *Paradise Lost* and *The Sorrows of Werter*, and experiences intense emotional swings that culminate in the creation of a sentient being. The creature’s actions—murder of William, the wrongful accusation of Justine, and subsequent wanderings among peasants—are recounted alongside Victor’s own wanderings in the Alps, his encounters with the creature, and his futile attempts to destroy it. The narrative moves through various locales—Oxford, Chamounix, and the icy seas—where Victor pursues the creature, only to lose it amid a violent storm.
