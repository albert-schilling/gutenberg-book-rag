The Wonderful Wizard of Oz is a modern‑style wonder tale written for children, presented as a series of short adventures that together form a single journey. The story opens with an introduction that explains the book’s aim to replace old fairy‑tale monsters with a happier, harmless fantasy. Dorothy, a Kansas girl swept away by a cyclone, meets a kindly old woman who sends her toward the Emerald City, the center of the magical land ruled by the mysterious Oz. Along the way she befriends a Scarecrow who longs for brains, a Tin Woodman who seeks a heart, and a Cowardly Lion who wants courage; together they face lions, wolves, poppy fields, and a wicked witch who melts when Dorothy throws water on her. Their travels are described in episodic chapters: building a raft, rescuing the Lion from poppies, staying in the palace, and receiving advice from the Great Humbug and other characters. The narrative moves toward the southern lands where they hope Glinda will help Dorothy return home. In the final chapter Dorothy awakens on the Kansas prairie, the silver shoes lost, and is joyfully reunited with Aunt Em and Uncle Henry. The book ends with her safe return to her ordinary world after a fantastical quest.
